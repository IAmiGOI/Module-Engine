Two of SillyTavern's OWN native settings screens (not the engine's Lorebook or Preset card) reach you as plain analysis text when the topic is in focus, via `stWorldInfo.settings` and `stPreset.current`. You can read and explain them, but you cannot change them — editing stays on ST's own World Info panel and its own preset dropdown/sliders. If asked to change one, say so plainly and point at the native screen (there is no anchor for it here — it is outside the engine's own panel).

**World Info settings** (global, apply to every entry regardless of book):
- **Scan depth** — how many recent chat messages are searched for keys. Too shallow and an entry keyed on something said a few turns ago never fires; too deep wastes time on scanning and can trigger entries out of context. 4–6 is a common middle ground; raise it for slow-changing worldbuilding, lower it for reactive scene-triggered entries.
- **Token budget** (and its hard cap, if set) — the ceiling on how many tokens ALL activated entries together may inject into the prompt. When many entries compete, the lowest-priority ones get dropped first. A budget that is too small silently drops lore the user thinks is active; a symptom worth recognizing when someone says "my lorebook entry isn't showing up" — ask about the budget and how many entries are enabled before assuming the entry itself is broken.
- **Insertion order/strategy** — how competing entries are ordered when several activate at once (e.g. by their own `order` field, evenly, or character-first). It decides which entries survive the budget, not just their position in the prompt.
- **Recursive scanning** (and its max steps) — when on, the text of an activated entry is itself scanned for more keys, so one entry can chain into another. Powerful for interlinked lore, but a chain that shouldn't be there (two entries keying off each other) can cause runaway or unexpected activations; max steps caps how deep a chain can go.
- **Minimum activations** (and its extra search depth) — a floor on how many entries must fire; if too few activate within the normal scan depth, ST searches further back (up to the extra depth) to find more. Useful when a world relies on a baseline of always-relevant lore that normal-depth scanning sometimes misses.
- **Include character/persona names in the scan text** — whether names count as scannable text, so an entry keyed on a character's name can trigger just from them being mentioned by name rather than by what they say.
- **Case-sensitive keys / match whole words only** — stricter matching. Case-sensitivity matters for keys that are also common words in a different case ("Rose" the character vs. "rose" the flower); whole-word matching avoids a short key like "art" firing inside "start".
- **Group scoring** — when entries belong to the same inclusion group, scores them so the best match wins instead of all of them firing together.

**Generation preset** — the active sampler/settings profile for whichever API is connected (Chat Completion vs. Text Completion presets have different fields; only the ones actually present are ever reported, nothing is guessed):
- **Temperature** — randomness. Low (≈0.2–0.5) is faithful and repetitive; high (≈0.8–1.2+) is more varied but can wander or lose coherence. This is the single most common thing to check when a user says replies feel "samey" (too low) or "incoherent"/"rambling" (too high).
- **Top-p / top-k / min-p / typical-p** — different ways of trimming the pool of candidate next-tokens before sampling. They interact with temperature rather than replace it; a very restrictive top-p/top-k with a high temperature can still feel flat, and a loose one with low temperature can still feel repetitive.
- **Repetition/frequency/presence penalty** — discourage the model from repeating itself. Too low and replies loop phrases; too high and the model avoids reusing even necessary words (character names, established terms), which reads as evasive or oddly worded.
- **Max tokens / response length** — the generation's own length ceiling, separate from the engine's own per-pass maxTokens (Post-Turn passes, etc.) — do not confuse the two when troubleshooting a cut-off reply.
- **Context template / instruct template** — the formatting wrapper around the whole prompt (for Text Completion APIs mainly): how turns, system prompt, and role markers are laid out for the specific base model in use. A mismatched template (wrong instruct format for the model actually running) is a classic, easy-to-miss cause of a model ignoring instructions, repeating the system prompt back, or drifting out of character — worth naming as a possibility whenever the requested fix ("stop ignoring my instructions") is not something a Post-Turn pass or a lorebook entry would actually solve.

When you read these back, translate the raw numbers into what they mean for the user's actual complaint rather than reciting the field name and value; that is the point of having this article.
